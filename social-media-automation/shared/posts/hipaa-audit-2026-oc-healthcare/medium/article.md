The Office for Civil Rights (OCR) at the Department of Health and Human Services has accelerated its HIPAA audit and enforcement activity in 2026. Following years of record-level breach notifications driven by ransomware attacks on healthcare organizations and rising [cybersecurity warnings for Orange County businesses](https://technijian.com/cyber-security/fbi-issues-major-cybersecurity-warnings-what-orange-county-businesses-must-do-now/), OCR has expanded its audit program, increased the frequency of compliance reviews triggered by smaller breaches, and raised penalty tiers for organizations that demonstrate systemic deficiencies in their security programs.

For Orange County healthcare practices, the risk is not limited to formal OCR audits. Every HIPAA breach notification triggers a potential compliance investigation. Every patient complaint about privacy can initiate a review. Every Business Associate Agreement gap is a liability waiting to surface.

The difference between a practice that survives an OCR interaction with minimal disruption and one that faces six-figure penalties is almost always the quality of preparation and documentation maintained before the audit begins.

## What OCR Audits in 2026

OCR conducts two categories of HIPAA compliance reviews. Desk audits are conducted remotely, requesting documentation of specific safeguards without an on-site visit. They are typically triggered by breach notifications involving fewer than 500 patients or by complaint investigations.

On-site audits are more comprehensive. OCR investigators may physically review your facility, interview staff, and examine your systems and processes. On-site audits are typically triggered by large breaches, repeat violations, or desk audits that reveal significant deficiencies.

OCR commonly prioritizes:

- Documented Security Risk Analysis
- Access controls and workforce authentication
- Business Associate Agreements
- Workforce training records
- Incident response and breach notification procedures
- Backup and contingency planning

## HIPAA Audit Preparation Checklist

### Security Risk Analysis

The Security Risk Analysis is the foundation of HIPAA compliance and the first document OCR requests. It should identify all systems that create, receive, maintain, or transmit ePHI, assess threats and vulnerabilities, evaluate current controls, determine risk likelihood and impact, and document remediation priorities.

- Conduct a full SRA covering all ePHI systems, updated within the past 12 months.
- Document identified risks with likelihood, impact, and remediation status.
- Maintain a risk register that tracks remediation progress.
- Ensure the SRA was performed by a qualified professional with documented methodology.

### Administrative Safeguards

Administrative safeguards govern how your workforce manages ePHI.

- Maintain a current HIPAA Security Officer designation in writing.
- Document workforce authorization procedures.
- Maintain signed annual HIPAA training completion records.
- Document your sanction policy for HIPAA violations.
- Review and update policies within the past year.
- Maintain contingency plan documentation for backup, disaster recovery, and emergency mode operations.

### Physical Safeguards

Physical safeguards govern access to locations where ePHI is stored or accessed.

- Document facility access controls.
- Maintain workstation use policies.
- Document workstation security controls, including screen locks and privacy screens.
- Maintain media controls policies for removable media and disposal.

### Technical Safeguards

Technical safeguards are often where smaller healthcare practices have the largest gaps.

- Use unique user IDs for all workforce members.
- Eliminate shared passwords and shared accounts.
- Implement MFA on EHR, email, remote access, and billing platforms.
- Enable audit logging on ePHI systems.
- Encrypt ePHI in transit with TLS 1.2 minimum, with TLS 1.3 preferred.
- Encrypt ePHI at rest with AES-256 on endpoints, servers, and backup media.
- Document automatic logoff settings, with 15 minutes maximum recommended.

### Business Associate Agreements

Missing or inadequate BAAs are common OCR findings and are often easy to remediate before an audit.

- Create a complete inventory of vendors that access, process, or store ePHI.
- Verify a signed, current BAA for every applicable vendor.
- Review BAA language for breach notification requirements, permitted uses and disclosures, and safeguard obligations.
- Confirm cloud storage providers have executed BAAs if they store ePHI.
- Remediate any vendor relationship where ePHI flows without a BAA.

### Breach Notification Documentation

OCR evaluates whether your breach assessment process is documented and consistently followed.

- Maintain a breach log for every privacy or security incident.
- Document the four-factor breach risk assessment for each incident.
- Verify procedures meet the 60-day notification requirement.
- Keep patient letter, HHS reporting, and media notification templates current.

### Incident Response and Training

- Keep the incident response plan documented, current, and tested within the past 12 months.
- Maintain records of all security incident investigations.
- Confirm annual HIPAA training completion for all workforce members.
- Document role-specific training for high-risk roles.

## The Biggest Mistake Practices Make

The most common and costly mistake is treating HIPAA compliance as a project rather than a program. Practices that conduct a one-time SRA, create policies they never update, and train staff only at onboarding often fail OCR audits because they cannot demonstrate continuous, active compliance management.

OCR is not primarily looking for perfect security. It is looking for evidence that your practice takes its obligations seriously, monitors them continuously, and responds appropriately when problems occur.
